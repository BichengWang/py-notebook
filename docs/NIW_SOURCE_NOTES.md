## 2. EB-2 baseline is separate from the NIW analysis

USCIS first considers eligibility for the underlying EB-2 classification. An NIW waives the job-offer requirement and, consequently, labor certification; it does **not** waive the EB-2 threshold. Self-petitioning is allowed. [U1, §§D, D.1–D.2; U2]

- **Advanced-degree professional route:** a qualifying U.S. advanced degree or foreign equivalent, or a qualifying bachelor's degree followed by at least five years of progressive post-degree experience in the specialty. The intended occupation through which the endeavor will be advanced must be a profession, ordinarily requiring at least a bachelor's degree for entry. Possessing an advanced degree alone is not enough; the manual explains the relationship of the experience, specialty and endeavor. [U1, §§A.2, D.1]
- **Exceptional-ability route:** expertise significantly above that ordinarily encountered in sciences, arts or business. USCIS uses an initial regulatory-criteria review and a separate final-merits review. At least three of six evidence categories must ordinarily be met: related academic record; employer letters documenting ten years of full-time occupational experience; professional license/certification; remuneration demonstrating exceptional ability; professional-association membership; and recognition of achievements/significant contributions. Comparable evidence is available when the criteria do not readily apply, with an explanation of comparability. Meeting three categories does not itself establish exceptional ability. The claimed ability must directly relate to the proposed endeavor. [U1, §§B.2, D.1]

**Missing facts, not a legal conclusion:** this research did not establish the individual's degrees/equivalency, progressive specialty experience, intended occupation's entry requirements, or an exceptional-ability record. Repository quality cannot stand in for those documents. No conclusion that either route is satisfied is warranted from this note.

## 3. Specific proposed endeavor and the three NIW prongs

USCIS applies the three-prong framework by a preponderance of the evidence, considers petitions case by case, and may grant a waiver as a matter of discretion. EB-2 classification alone does not establish NIW eligibility. [U1, §D.2]

### Prong 1: substantial merit and national importance

An **endeavor is more specific than an occupation**. Describe the actual projects, goals, technical domain, intended U.S. activities, direct potential impacts and implementation/dissemination route, rather than stating “work in AI” or listing software-engineer duties. Substantial merit can exist without immediate or quantifiable economic returns; national importance separately concerns the specific endeavor's potential prospective impact. [U1, §§D.1, D.3, first prong]

For this portfolio, a possible framing is **developing and evaluating reproducible decision-model controls for specified U.S. commerce workflows**, such as offer/ranking evaluation, experiment release decisions and approval-constrained actions. This is a drafting hypothesis, not the individual's confirmed plan. Specify target users, what will be developed, the person's contribution, evaluation milestones, resource requirements and how knowledge or tools could transfer beyond one employer.

The importance of AI, a STEM label, an occupational shortage, or an employer's nationwide footprint does not by itself establish the endeavor's national importance. USCIS expressly distinguishes benefits confined to an employer or its clients from broader implications. It gives examples of relevant evidence such as widespread adoption/licensing interest, important novel operational processes, and effects on development of similar technologies by others. Geographic reach alone is not the test. [U1, §D.3, first prong; §D.4]

**U.S. commerce/AI-controls relevance is a hypothesis, not established national impact.** NIST discusses AI's potential benefits and risks in commerce and supports context-specific risk management. That establishes a relevant problem space, not that this repository meaningfully affects U.S. commerce, competitiveness, public welfare or national security. A defensible causal argument needs evidence connecting the specific controls to a documented U.S. need, credible prospective use, measurable outcomes and broader transferability. Actual deployment is not a universal NIW prerequisite, but a synthetic demonstration cannot be presented as realized national benefit. [N1, Executive Summary; U1, §D.3]

### Prong 2: well positioned to advance the endeavor

USCIS considers education/skills and relevant success, a detailed future plan, progress already made, and interest or support from users, customers, investors or other relevant entities. Its examples include publications about the person's work, citations/adoption, contracts or licenses, feasible funding plans, and evidence of use by others. The list is **neither a mandatory checklist nor a statement that any single item is sufficient**. Ultimate success need not be shown more likely than not, but unsubstantiated assertions do not meet the evidentiary burden. [U1, §D.3, second prong]

Repository releases, reproducible artifacts and documented contribution history can help describe technical progress. They do not independently prove who performed the work, real-world efficacy, adoption or field influence. Corroborate authorship and role, relevant prior achievements, a feasible U.S. work plan, resources and genuine external interest. Expert letters are more persuasive when grounded in first-hand knowledge, specific examples and other independent evidence; generic praise is not a substitute. STEM degrees can be positive factors when tied to the endeavor, but a degree alone is insufficient. [U1, §§D.3–D.4]

### Prong 3: the balance favors waiving job offer/labor certification

This is a separate argument, not “AI is important, therefore waive.” USCIS balances the proposed contributions against labor certification's protection of U.S. job opportunities, wages and working conditions. Relevant considerations may include impracticality of labor certification given the person's qualifications or endeavor, benefits even if other U.S. workers are available, substantiated urgency, or unique skills not appropriately captured by minimum occupational requirements. Economic impact and job creation can be relevant; an occupational shortage alone does not satisfy this prong. [U1, §D.3, third prong]

**Missing facts:** the individual's actual work arrangement, reasons a waiver would benefit the United States, evidence of urgency or distinctive contribution, and implications for U.S. workers are not established here. Do not claim that entrepreneurship, open publication or AI work automatically resolves the balance. Government/quasi-government letters can help if genuine and specific, but are not required. [U1, §§D.3, D.5–D.6]

## 4. Technical evidence: useful NIST crosswalk, not certification

AI RMF is **voluntary**, context-dependent and not a fixed compliance checklist. Its four functions are GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE and MANAGE. The following are recommended evidence mappings, not claims that the portfolio fully implements the framework. [N1, Executive Summary and §5; N2]

| Evidence to preserve in a technical report/release | Primary-source basis |
| --- | --- |
| Intended use and excluded uses; target population/workflow; assumptions; risks; human oversight and responsibility boundaries | MAP 1.1, 2.2–2.3, 3.3, 3.5; GOVERN 2.1, 3.2 |
| Test-data provenance and representativeness; held-out evaluation; metrics, tools, baselines, uncertainty and exclusions; complete results including failures | MEASURE §5.3 narrative, 2.1, 2.3, 2.5; N1 §3.1. Exact version identifiers, hashes and rerun instructions are portfolio recommendations for repeatability, not a NIST-mandated file format. |
| Assessment of security, privacy, harmful bias and unmeasured risks; known generalization limits | MEASURE 1.1, 2.5, 2.7, 2.10–2.11. Synthetic category slices or forbidden-feature checks alone are not proof of comprehensive fairness or privacy. |
| Clear separation of developer-produced evaluation from outside assessment; reviewer identity, qualifications, relationship/conflicts, methods and artifacts examined | MEASURE 1.3; §5.3 narrative notes independent review can mitigate internal biases/conflicts. The framework also permits internal experts who were not front-line developers; do not equate that with external independence. |
| Go/no-go decisions, policy gates, approval/override paths, residual risks, incident response and monitoring plans | MANAGE 1.1, 1.4, 2.4, 4.1; GOVERN 1.5. A replay artifact can show what was recorded, not necessarily that all real-world actions or harms were captured. |
| Third-party model/data/software inventory, relevant permissions and supply-chain limitations | GOVERN 1.1, 6.1; MAP 4.1–4.2; N1 §3.4. Repository licensing does not itself clear upstream models, data or outputs. |

NIST explicitly warns that laboratory risk measurements can differ from operational settings. Accuracy claims should use realistic test sets representative of expected use and documented methods; generalization limits must be recorded. Passing software tests or release gates establishes only the tested behavior, not production model quality, safety, adoption or national benefit. [N1, §§1.2.1, 3.1; N2, MEASURE 2.3–2.5]

The portfolio overview describes RELIA as synthetic-data evaluation, experiment-gate as A/B decision logic, JEV as typed decisions with offline evaluation, and decision-control as approval/evidence controls. The inspected RELIA specification expressly says its gates are proposed engineering gates, not government/industry standards, and that its synthetic comparisons do not establish statistical superiority or external validity. Retain those boundaries in the report. These are repository descriptions, not independently verified implementation or deployment findings. [Repository: `ai-workflows/README.md`, Current projects; `ai-workflows/ai-decision-reliability/docs/SPEC.md`, §§5, 7, 9]

Where generative AI is actually used, the NIST profile highlights confabulation, automation bias and information integrity. A typed response or confidence-like score is not itself evidence of correctness or calibrated decision confidence. Human approval does not automatically prevent over-reliance. [N4, §§2.2, 2.7]

## 5. Truthful publication posture and independence

NIST's information-integrity discussion emphasizes distinguishing fact from opinion/inference, acknowledging uncertainty, linking to original sources and accurately representing the level of vetting. USCIS calls for corroboration of projections and independent support for plans, and treats published recognition in context rather than as an automatic qualification. [N4, §2.8; U1, §§D.3, D.6, “Published Materials” and “Letters and Other Statements from Third Parties”]

Practical release/report rules derived from that guidance:

- Label author-produced technical reports, first-party announcements, paid/syndicated releases and generated summaries as such. They can document methods, plans and dissemination; they are **not independent validation merely because they appear on another website**. The manual does not prohibit author-produced evidence; its weight and corroboration are the issue.
- A model-generated review or another AI agent's review is not an independent human expert endorsement. Do not invent reviewer identities or imply peer review, external replication or editorial selection that did not occur.
- Separate **implemented capability**, **observed test result**, **proposed work**, **prospective benefit**, and **externally corroborated outcome**. Include scope, data type, execution environment and limitations with any metric; identify frozen replay responses versus live model calls.
- Claim a public release, deployment, customer use, third-party review or publication only after retaining corresponding records. A drafted announcement is not publication; public code availability is not adoption. Do not turn projected savings or risk reduction into achieved outcomes.
- Prefer “mapped to selected NIST AI RMF outcomes” with specific evidence and gaps over “NIST-certified,” “government-approved,” or blanket compliance/safety claims. NIST's publications expressly disclaim implied endorsement of mentioned products; citation is not endorsement. [N1 and N4, front matter]
- Do not assert licensing clearance, permission to disclose private evidence, or immigration eligibility from this note. Preserve third-party rights/permissions review separately from technical evaluation. [N2, GOVERN 6.1 and MAP 4.1]

## 6. Recommended evidence gaps for the final report

These are **facts not established in this research scope**, not findings that no such evidence exists or that the person is legally ineligible:

1. **EB-2 threshold:** authenticated educational/equivalency and specialty-experience records, or the applicable exceptional-ability criteria plus final-merits evidence; intended occupation and its entry requirements. [U1, §§B.2, D.1]
2. **Endeavor definition:** the person's confirmed, specific U.S. plan, role, milestones, funding/resources and intended beneficiaries; avoid presenting the drafting hypothesis above as a commitment. [U1, §D.3]
3. **Independent corroboration:** identifiable reviewers with disclosed relationships and actual assessment records; external reproduction, genuine user/customer correspondence, adoption/citation evidence, or relevant agreements where available. No single category is universally required. [U1, §§D.3, D.6; N2, MEASURE 1.3]
4. **Technical external validity:** representative real-world evaluation where authorized; independent labels and baseline comparisons; uncertainty, safety/privacy/security/bias assessment and residual-risk boundaries. Preserve synthetic evidence honestly in the meantime. [N1, §§1.2.1, 3.1; N2, MEASURE]
5. **Broader U.S. implications:** documentary support for the specific commerce problem, evidence that the approach could transfer beyond one client/employer, credible adoption interest and a supported prospective-impact argument. NIST relevance is not a national-importance finding. [U1, §D.3, first prong]
6. **Waiver balance:** person- and endeavor-specific facts about labor-certification practicality, benefits despite available U.S. workers, any evidenced urgency and worker effects. Do not reuse the prong-one narrative as proof of prong three. [U1, §D.3, third prong]
7. **Publication/release records:** actual publication URLs/dates, author/sponsor/editorial disclosures, artifact identity and run records, third-party permissions and limits on sharing private evidence. Do not claim news coverage from syndication alone. [N4, §2.8; U1, §D.6; N2, GOVERN 6.1]
